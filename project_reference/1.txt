1. Executive Summary
Institutional kitchens across India — hotels, colleges, hospitals, banquet halls, and mess facilities — generate large volumes of surplus food every day. Most of this surplus is never tracked, verified, or redistributed in time, and a large share of it ends up mixed with spoiled waste and sent to landfill. This is both an economic loss for the kitchen and a missed opportunity to feed people in need.
This project proposes an AI-powered food waste management and redistribution platform that predicts surplus before it is cooked, verifies and grades edible surplus in real time, matches it to nearby verified NGOs through geospatial routing, and diverts anything unsafe to composting, Bio-CNG, or Black Soldier Fly (BSFL) processing instead of a landfill. The platform is built to be technically feasible on a lean, open-source stack, economically viable for both kitchens and the platform operator, and commercially monetizable through a SaaS subscription plus a biomass brokerage revenue stream.
At scale, the platform targets an 8–15% reduction in kitchen food-procurement costs, over 92% accuracy in surplus-demand forecasting, NGO matching within 10 minutes, and a fully digital, FSSAI-2019-compliant audit trail for every handoff — turning a compliance liability under the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 into a measurable, revenue-generating sustainability program.
2. Problem Statement
Hotels, colleges, hospitals, and mess facilities generate significant daily food surplus, but no centralized system exists to track, verify, and redistribute edible food before it spoils. This creates four compounding problems:
2.1 Massive Economic Bleed
Commercial kitchens lose an estimated 10–15% of their monthly food budget to over-preparation and inaccurate demand estimation. At a national level, this contributes to India's 78-million-tonne annual food waste problem, valued at roughly ₹1.55 lakh crore per year.
2.2 No Real-Time Coordination
There is no centralized system to track, verify, or redistribute edible surplus in real time. NGOs and composting units operate without visibility into what surplus exists, where it is, or how long it will remain safe to eat — so donations are fragmented and ad hoc rather than systematic.
2.3 Legal & Compliance Risk
Under the Solid Waste Management (SWM) Rules, 2016, Bulk Waste Generators face municipal fines ranging from ₹5,000 to ₹25,000 per day for dumping wet waste into landfills instead of processing or diverting it responsibly. Spoiled food is frequently mixed with edible surplus, increasing both landfill volume and regulatory exposure.
2.4 Perishable Decay Window
Surplus food typically remains within the FSSAI-defined safety window for only 2–4 hours after preparation. Without real-time coordination between kitchens, NGOs, and processing units, essentially all of that edible surplus turns into landfill waste before anyone can act on it.
3. Proposed Solution
An AI-powered platform that prevents surplus before it happens, and responsibly redistributes whatever surplus remains:
* Predicts surplus food using historical consumption and wastage data, so kitchens can prepare closer to actual demand.
* Detects and monitors leftover food quantity and freshness in real time using quality-verification checks.
* Matches edible food with nearby NGOs based on need, location, and pickup capacity using geospatial search.
* Dynamically allocates pickups and enables emergency redistribution when volumes spike.
* Routes spoiled or unsafe food to composting, fertilizer, Bio-CNG, or BSFL (Black Soldier Fly) processing units instead of landfill.
* Provides real-time pickup tracking, notifications, and waste analytics with a full digital audit trail.
3.1 Six-Step Redistribution Workflow
* Food Data Collection — hotels, colleges, hospitals, and mess facilities enter surplus food details through the platform.
* AI Surplus Prediction — the model analyzes historical consumption and wastage data to predict potential food surplus before it occurs.
* Food Quality Verification — the system assesses food condition using quality and safety information against the FSSAI decay window.
* Decision Engine (“Is the food edible?”) — routes the item down one of two paths:
   * Edible route: NGO Matching & Notification → NGO Acceptance → Pickup & Distribution Confirmation, with confirmed collection and routing to the community.
   * Spoiled route: Composting / Fertilizer Unit — unsafe or spoiled food is redirected for composting or agricultural use.

4.2 System Architecture
User-facing apps (Flutter/Dart) for donors and beneficiaries communicate with a Node.js server, which reads and writes to a MongoDB data lake holding food and logistics data. Three downstream integrations sit on top of this core:
* Government Compliance and Audit Reports — a dedicated API that can be integrated directly into government systems for regulatory reporting.
* Donor and NGO Redistribution Apps — end-user, multi-platform mobile applications for donors and NGOs.
* Donor Dashboards and Web Portal — a browser-based extension and dashboard for donor organizations.
4.3 Two-Stage AI-Driven Waste Prevention & Logistics Model
Stage 1 — Waste Prevention (Plate Count Prediction)
Using calendar, weather, and booking data as input, an AI demand-forecast model predicts total daily plates required, producing a predicted daily plate requirement as its Stage 1 output. A decision check (“Extra or spoiled food detected?”) determines whether Stage 2 is triggered.
Stage 2 — Extra & Spoiled Food Redistribution
When surplus or spoiled food is detected, three parallel sub-systems run:
* Preprocessing & Quality Check — parses incoming reports, verifies protocols, and runs food/face detection and cropping steps for image-based quality checks.
* Model Evaluation — confusion matrix, performance metrics, and error analysis feed into an export routing plan, which is deployed and loaded as the trained model for production use.
* Logistics Optimization & Predictive Model — an adversarial network and dynamic routing model work alongside a perishable-decay estimator, a donor-reliability score, a delivery ETA forecast, and audio-visual anomaly detection to decide the optimal, safest route for each item.
The KEY on the architecture diagram distinguishes a Prediction Flow (dashed) from the Training Flow / Process Flow (solid) that continuously improves the models over time.

9. Conclusion
India loses an estimated 78 million tonnes of food every year, worth approximately ₹1.55 lakh crore — much of it from institutional kitchens that have no reliable way to predict, verify, or redistribute their own surplus before it spoils. This platform addresses that gap end-to-end: it prevents over-preparation with AI demand forecasting, verifies and routes edible surplus to verified NGOs within minutes using geospatial matching, and diverts anything unsafe to composting and biomass processing instead of a landfill — all under a fully digital, FSSAI-2019-compliant audit trail.
With a lean, open-source technology stack, a clear path to positive unit economics (roughly 8x ROI and a 30-day payback for customers), and a monetization model that pairs recurring SaaS revenue with biomass brokerage income, the platform is designed to be technically feasible, economically viable, and commercially sustainable from day one — while delivering measurable economic, environmental, social, and compliance impact to every kitchen it serves.
